The fastest way to extend human life is to accelerate the science. Not incrementally — fundamentally. Autonomous labs that run thousands of experiments while you sleep, robots that pursue hypotheses no human thought to test.

What we mean by autonomous labs

An autonomous lab is not remote-controlled equipment. It is a system that forms hypotheses, designs experiments, executes them, interprets results, and decides what to try next — without waiting for a human in the loop.

Beyond task completion

Most lab automation today executes a fixed protocol. You tell it what to do, it does it, and it stops. That is useful, but it does not accelerate discovery. It accelerates throughput on a single line of inquiry.

Exploratory robotics answers a different question: what should we try next, given everything we've seen so far? The lab becomes a reasoning system, not just an execution system.

How it works

Each experiment generates three outputs:

  • Results — the data you asked for
  • Anomalies — unexpected signals the system flagged for follow-up
  • Next experiments — autonomously generated protocols to pursue the most promising leads

The lab does not stop when your prompt is complete. It stops when the science is exhausted.

This is critical for longevity. If we are going to extend all of human life to the age of 100, science needs to move faster than any single researcher — or any single prompt — can drive it.

Why a guaranteed healthy hundred

Average global life expectancy is 73. In wealthy countries it is 80. Neither number reflects what biology allows — it reflects what current medicine delivers.

The gap is infrastructure, not knowledge

We understand the major mechanisms of aging: cellular senescence, telomere attrition, mitochondrial dysfunction, loss of proteostasis. Interventions exist for each. What doesn't exist is a system that can test, combine, and optimize those interventions at the speed required to change outcomes in our lifetime.

Why autonomous labs close the gap

A single researcher can run a handful of experiments per week. An autonomous lab can run thousands per day, each informed by the results of the last. The compounding effect is not linear — it is exponential discovery.

The question is not whether healthy longevity is achievable. It is whether we can build the infrastructure to get there fast enough. That is what Bunker is.

The default human lifespan should be a healthy hundred years.
