A causal map is not a diagram. It is a computational model of how a disease initiates, progresses, defends itself, and can be interrupted.

## Beyond correlation

Most disease research identifies associations. Gene X is correlated with outcome Y. Biomarker Z predicts progression. These are useful, but they do not tell you where to intervene.

A causal map answers a different question: **if we change this variable, what happens downstream?** That requires modeling the mechanism, not just measuring the association.

## How we build them

Each map starts from three data layers:

- **Genomic**: driver mutations, regulatory networks, expression profiles
- **Proteomic**: signaling cascades, protein interactions, post-translational modifications
- **Tissue-level**: spatial organization, microenvironment dynamics, immune landscape

These layers are integrated into a single computational model that can be queried counterfactually: *what happens if we block this pathway? What if we activate this receptor?*

## Why publish them

Our maps are published as they are built. This is not altruism — it is methodology. Eradication requires that claims be verifiable and mechanisms be reproducible. A proprietary map that cannot be challenged is a liability, not an asset.

Every program page will carry its data. Every claim on this site carries its source.
